Chapter 8 · 8.2 Storing UTF-8 Encoded Text with Strings

Bytes, scalar values, and grapheme clusters

From Rust’s perspective there are three relevant ways to look at strings. Take the Hindi word “नमस्ते”, written in the Devanagari script.

Bytes — stored as a vector of u8 values: 18 bytes, how computers ultimately store this data

[224, 164, 168, 224, 164, 174, 224, 164, 184, 224, 165, 141, 224, 164, 164,

224, 165, 135]

Unicode scalar values (Rust’s char type): six char values, but the fourth and sixth are not letters — they’re diacritics that don’t make sense on their own

['न', 'म', 'स', '्', 'त', 'े']

Grapheme clusters: what a person would call the four letters that make up the Hindi word

["न", "म", "स्", "ते"]

Definition 8.7 (Grapheme cluster). The way of looking at a string that comes closest to what we would call its letters.
