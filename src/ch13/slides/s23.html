Chapter 13 · 13.1 Closures

The three Fn traits

Definition 13.4 (FnOnce). Applies to closures that can be called once. All closures implement at least this trait, because all closures can be called. A closure that moves captured values out of its body implements only FnOnce and none of the other Fn traits, because it can only be called once.

Definition 13.5 (FnMut). Applies to closures that don’t move captured values out of their body but might mutate the captured values. These closures can be called more than once.

Definition 13.6 (Fn). Applies to closures that don’t move captured values out of their body and don’t mutate captured values, as well as closures that capture nothing from their environment. These closures can be called more than once without mutating their environment, which is important in cases such as calling a closure multiple times concurrently.

The traits are additive: every Fn closure is also FnMut, and every FnMut closure is also FnOnce.
