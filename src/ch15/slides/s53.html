Chapter 15 · 15.5 RefCell<T> and the Interior Mutability Pattern

Enforcing borrowing rules at runtime

Unlike Rc<T>, the RefCell<T> type represents single ownership over the data it holds. So what makes it different from a type like Box<T>? Recall the borrowing rules of Chapter 4:

  • At any given time, you can have either one mutable reference or any number of immutable references (but not both).
  • References must always be valid.

With references and Box<T>, these invariants are enforced at compile time; break them and you get a compiler error. With RefCell<T>, they are enforced at runtime; break them and your program will panic and exit.

Checking at compile time catches errors sooner in the development process, with no impact on runtime performance, because all the analysis is completed beforehand. That makes it the best choice in the majority of cases, which is why it is Rust’s default.
