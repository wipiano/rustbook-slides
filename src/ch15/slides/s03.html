Chapter 15 · 15.1 Using Box<T> to Point to Data on the Heap

Boxes and when to use them

Definition 15.2 (Box). The most straightforward smart pointer, of type Box<T>. A box stores data on the heap rather than the stack; what remains on the stack is the pointer to the heap data (stack vs. heap: Chapter 4).

Boxes have no performance overhead other than storing their data on the heap instead of the stack — but they don’t have many extra capabilities either. You’ll use them most often in these situations:

  1. When you have a type whose size can’t be known at compile time, and you want to use a value of that type in a context that requires an exact size.
  2. When you have a large amount of data, and you want to transfer ownership but ensure that the data won’t be copied when you do so.
  3. When you want to own a value, and you care only that it’s a type that implements a particular trait rather than being of a specific type.
