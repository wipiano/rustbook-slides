Chapter 21 · 21.2 From a Single-Threaded to a Multithreaded Server

Sending requests to threads via channels

The closures given to thread::spawn do absolutely nothing. We get the closure to execute in execute, but we must give thread::spawn a closure when we create each Worker during the creation of the ThreadPool. We want each Worker to fetch the code to run from a queue held in the ThreadPool and send it to its thread to run.

The channels of Chapter 16 — a simple way to communicate between two threads — are perfect for this. A channel functions as the queue of jobs, and execute sends a job from the ThreadPool to the Worker instances, which send it to their thread. The plan:

  1. The ThreadPool creates a channel and holds on to the sender.
  2. Each Worker holds on to the receiver.
  3. A new Job struct holds the closures we want to send down the channel.
  4. The execute method sends the job it wants to execute through the sender.
  5. In its thread, the Worker loops over its receiver and executes the closures of any jobs it receives.
