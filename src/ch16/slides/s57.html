We did it! Counting from 0 to 10 may not seem very impressive, but it taught us a lot about Mutex<T> and thread safety. This program’s structure can also do more complicated operations than incrementing a counter: divide a calculation into independent parts, split those parts across threads, and then use a Mutex<T> to have each thread update the final result with its part.

Remark. For simple numerical operations, there are types simpler than Mutex<T> in the std::sync::atomic module of the standard library. They provide safe, concurrent, atomic access to primitive types. This example used Mutex<T> with a primitive type to concentrate on how Mutex<T> works.
