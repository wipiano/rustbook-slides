Chapter 16 · Introduction

A variety of tools, not one dogma

Remark. For simplicity’s sake, the book refers to many of the problems as concurrent rather than being more precise by saying concurrent and/or parallel. For this chapter, mentally substitute concurrent and/or parallel whenever it says concurrent. In the next chapter, where the distinction matters more, it is more specific.

Many languages are dogmatic about the solutions they offer for handling concurrent problems. For example, Erlang has elegant functionality for message-passing concurrency but has only obscure ways to share state between threads.

  • Supporting only a subset of possible solutions is a reasonable strategy for higher-level languages, because a higher-level language promises benefits from giving up some control to gain abstractions.
  • Lower-level languages, however, are expected to provide the solution with the best performance in any given situation, and have fewer abstractions over the hardware.

Therefore, Rust offers a variety of tools for modeling problems in whatever way is appropriate for your situation and requirements: threads, message passing, shared state, and the Send and Sync traits.
