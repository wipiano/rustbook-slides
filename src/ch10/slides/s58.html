Chapter 10 · 10.3 Validating References with Lifetimes

Lifetimes: another kind of generic

Lifetimes are another kind of generic that we’ve already been using. Rather than ensuring that a type has the behavior we want, lifetimes ensure that references are valid as long as we need them to be.

Definition 10.10 (Lifetime). The scope for which a reference is valid. Every reference in Rust has a lifetime — a detail not discussed in “References and Borrowing” (Chapter 4).

Most of the time, lifetimes are implicit and inferred, just as most of the time types are inferred. We must annotate types only when multiple types are possible; similarly, we must annotate lifetimes when the lifetimes of references could be related in a few different ways. Rust requires us to annotate the relationships using generic lifetime parameters to ensure that the actual references used at runtime will definitely be valid.

Remark. Annotating lifetimes is not even a concept most other programming languages have, so this will feel unfamiliar. The chapter doesn’t cover lifetimes in their entirety, but discusses common ways you might encounter lifetime syntax, so you can get comfortable with the concept.
